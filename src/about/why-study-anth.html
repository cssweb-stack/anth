Why Study Anthropology?

Explore human lives across cultures, environments and time. The Department of Anthropology at the University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa brings together archaeology, biological anthropology and cultural anthropology, with opportunities to connect classroom study with fieldwork, research and community relationships in Hawaiʻi, the Pacific and Asia.

Undergraduate Study

Undergraduate studies in anthropology at the University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa explore and develop an understanding of ancient and contemporary diversity in human biology, behavior, culture, and society through comparative, holistic studies of communities. The curriculum in archaeology, biological, and cultural anthropology prepares students to develop meaningful careers in an increasingly global and multicultural world.

Graduate Study

Graduate studies in anthropology at the University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa are oriented toward training individuals to become well-rounded scholars and teachers, as well as competent researchers and/or practitioners in one or more subfields of anthropology (archaeology, biological anthropology, and cultural anthropology).

Student Organizations & Mentoring

Lambda Alpha National Anthropology Honor Society

Lambda Alpha recognizes academic achievement among undergraduate and graduate anthropology students.

Anthropology Undergraduate Student Association (AUSA)

AUSA connects undergraduate majors and non-majors through anthropology, networking, community service and research opportunities.

Anthropology Graduate Student Association (AGSA)

AGSA supports graduate student research, professional development and communication within the department.

Undergraduate Mentoring Program (UMP)

Graduate mentors and undergraduate mentees explore anthropology, coursework and graduate student life together.
