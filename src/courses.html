Anthropology (ANTH) Courses

Anthropology explores the human experience across cultures, societies, and time, examining how people have evolved, adapted, and shaped the world around them. At the University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa, students investigate human origins, cultural traditions, languages, social relationships, and the connections between people and their environments. The curriculum brings particular depth to the study of Hawaiʻi, the Pacific, and Asia while engaging with diverse societies and perspectives worldwide. Through archaeological investigation, cultural research, biological inquiry, and applied learning, students develop the knowledge and skills to understand human diversity, examine contemporary challenges, and appreciate the complexity of human life.

Lower-Division Courses (100–299)

Lower-division courses introduce the fundamental concepts and methods used to understand humanity across time and place. Students explore human evolution, cultural diversity, archaeological discoveries, and the ways societies create meaning, organize communities, and interact with their environments. Courses also examine the cultural and ecological history of Hawaiʻi, Polynesian traditions, and the relationships between human behavior, biology, and society. Through introductory research methods, quantitative reasoning, and critical inquiry, students begin developing the skills to investigate human experiences and recognize the many ways people adapt to and shape their worlds.

{% endcall %} {% call imageTextRow('/media/units/anth/courses/course-02.webp', 600, 600) %}

Upper-Division Courses (300–499)

Upper-division courses offer opportunities to investigate human societies, cultures, and biological diversity through specialized study and practical research. Students explore archaeology, human evolution, language, health, religion, gender, economic systems, and the relationships between communities and their environments. Regional studies examine Hawaiʻi, the Pacific, Asia, and societies throughout the world, while specialized topics address Indigenous knowledge, colonialism, cultural change, environmental sustainability, and contemporary social challenges. Fieldwork, archaeological excavation, laboratory analysis, ethnographic research, and community engagement allow students to connect anthropological theory with practice while developing a deeper understanding of the ethical responsibilities involved in studying human lives and histories.

{% endcall %} {% call imageTextRow('/media/units/anth/courses/course-03.webp', 600, 600) %}

Graduate Courses (600–699)

Graduate courses provide advanced study of anthropological theory, research methods, and the biological, cultural, linguistic, and archaeological dimensions of human experience. Students engage with contemporary scholarship while investigating subjects such as human evolution, cultural change, health and medicine, environmental relationships, and the social forces shaping communities. Specialized studies emphasize Hawaiʻi, the Pacific, and Asia alongside comparative and global perspectives. Through advanced field and laboratory methods, critical analysis, and applied research, students develop the theoretical and methodological foundations for independent scholarship and professional practice, with attention to research ethics, Indigenous perspectives, and community responsibilities.

{% endcall %} {% call imageTextRow('/media/units/anth/courses/course-04.webp', 600, 600) %}

Advanced Graduate Study (700–800)

Advanced graduate study emphasizes specialized scholarship, independent inquiry, and original research across the major fields of anthropology. Through research seminars and sustained investigation, students deepen their expertise in archaeological, biological, cultural, and ethnographic approaches while examining complex questions about human societies, histories, and environments. Advanced training in research design, participatory methods, data analysis, and scholarly writing supports the development of rigorous and ethically informed research. Master's thesis and doctoral dissertation work provide opportunities to pursue original investigations, contribute new knowledge to the discipline, and advance understanding of the human experience.

{% endcall %}
