Graduate Degrees Awarded

Generations of graduate students in the Department of Anthropology at the University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa have contributed to the study of human lives, cultures, and histories. Their work reflects a longstanding engagement with Hawaiʻi, Asia, and the Pacific, alongside research in communities around the world. These degree records bring together the people and projects behind that continuing tradition of scholarship.

{% call imageTextRow('', tile={label: 'MA', color: 'tile-purple'}) %}

Master of Arts (MA) Degree Recipients

MA degree records extend back to 1930 and include both thesis and non-thesis graduates. Research titles range from archaeological sites, artifacts, and settlement patterns to family life, migration, identity, religion, and health. Studies of material culture, human biology, and relationships with the environment illustrate the varied questions students have pursued through master’s study.

Doctor of Philosophy (PhD) Degree Recipients

Dissertations recorded since 1967 investigate how societies change, how communities sustain knowledge and traditions, and how people negotiate livelihoods, land, health, and belonging. Research across cultural, archaeological, biological, medical, and ecological anthropology connects detailed studies of communities and landscapes with broader questions about power, human adaptation, and the past. Together, these dissertations document decades of sustained anthropological inquiry.
