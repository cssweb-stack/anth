PhD: Publication

The dissertation by publication option brings published research articles together as a cohesive contribution to anthropology.

Guidelines

Guidelines for PhD by publication:

  1. The decision to use a dissertation-by-article option must be made and signed off on by the entire dissertation committee, usually during comprehensive examinations or during the proposal defense. This timing allows for the proper development of a plan of action.
  2. A minimum of three accepted or published peer-reviewed articles are required for the completion of the degree. Revise decisions, in and of themselves, do not meet the requirements of this option. All revisions undertaken must be completed and accepted by the journal editor.
  3. Book chapters, conference proceedings, research notes, book reviews, encyclopedia entries, appendices, and unpublished reports do not count toward the three-article minimum.
  4. Submission of the dissertation should include an introduction and conclusion describing the overarching framework in the research and the synergistic qualities of the three articles. Connections between the three articles must be explicit and clear, as judged by the dissertation committee. The introduction should serve to justify the grouping of the three articles while the conclusion should develop the major contributions of the entire project.
  5. All articles must be the result of original research by the student undertaken while the PhD student was enrolled at UH Mānoa. The research should be problem-oriented and anthropologically relevant as judged by members of the student’s committee. No articles derived from an MA thesis can count toward the three-article minimum.
  6. Co-authorship of articles is acceptable. The student must be the lead author of any co-authored pieces. Committee members may be co-authors of articles that contribute to a dissertation by publication. A signed document indicating that the student undertook most of the work on an article is required of all co-authors. The student must be responsible for the conceptualization, analysis, and writing of each article. They do not need to be responsible for the funding of the project.
  7. The journals to which the student submits work must be chosen in consultation with the dissertation committee. The journals must be appropriate to the field and at least two need to be published in recognized international journals. Students may publish in open-access journals if they have the funds to do so. However, care should be taken to avoid predatory and near-predatory publishers in consultation with the chair of the student’s dissertation committee. Students and committees should strive to publish the three articles in three different journals.
  8. Datasets used but not published in full must be included at the end of the dissertation by publication in an appendix or made available online in connection with a publication.
  9. The students may wish to include material that is unpublished beyond the three-article minimum. In this case, the student needs to make clear in the introduction what components of the dissertation are not yet published.
  10. Students must read publication contracts closely to ensure the student can use the article in a dissertation. While most journals will allow this practice, students must be cognizant that some journals do not allow publication with ProQuest. Students must secure written permission from any co-authors and all copyright holders to include articles in their dissertation.
  11. Current students are eligible for this form of dissertation. If interested, students must formally consult with their committee as soon as possible. While a new proposal will not be required, students will need to provide a document describing how their three proposed articles will address a cohesive anthropological and problem-oriented question.
  12. Students must conform to any and all other Graduate Division guidelines regarding the inclusion of previously published material in dissertations.
