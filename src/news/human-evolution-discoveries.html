Professor Christopher Bae of the University of Hawaiʻi is at the forefront of groundbreaking discoveries in human evolution. His recent identification of a new human species in Eastern Asia, dating back 50,000 years, challenges existing classifications and sheds light on gaps in Asia’s fossil record. Through his research and participation in paleoanthropology conferences, Bae advocates for Asian-centered approaches to understanding human origins, questioning long-standing Western models. His personal journey—from being adopted as a child in Korea to becoming a leading researcher—mirrors his scientific pursuit of piecing together humanity’s past. With ongoing projects across the region, his work continues to reshape our understanding of early human history.

Tackling Asia's 'Muddle in the Middle'

Video Overview: Professor Bae discusses his recent research and participation in paleoanthropology conferences in China. His work challenges Western models of human evolution by advocating for Asian-centered approaches to interpreting fossil records. Bae highlights the need for re-evaluating classifications, including the debated "middle Paleolithic" in China and the introduction of Homo longi and Homo juluis as distinct species. He also shares his personal journey, from being adopted as a child in Korea to becoming a leading researcher in East Asian anthropology. With ongoing projects across Asia, Bae anticipates further discoveries that could reshape our understanding of human evolution.
